01 · The Object

A vessel that happens to be a speaker.

We began with a question that audio engineers rarely ask. Not "how do we make it louder," nor "how do we make it flatter" — but how do we make a speaker that is welcome in a room when it is silent? The answer, it turned out, lived four centuries away.

Arita porcelain is dense, internally damped, and acoustically inert in ways that wood and aluminum can only approximate. The wall, six millimeters thick, behaves as if it isn't there. The driver, a paper-coned full-range, sings into a chamber that returns nothing of itself.

What remains is the recording. Nothing else.

02 · Principles

Four quiet decisions.

— I

One driver.

A single full-range paper cone handles every frequency a human can hear. No crossover, no time-smear, no compromise between voices. The recording arrives as one continuous gesture.

— II

A clay cabinet.

Six-millimeter Arita stoneware, internally damped by its own mass. The cabinet does not contribute to the sound. It refuses to.

— III

A linear response.

40 Hz to 20 kHz, ±2 dB on-axis. Phase coherent across the entire passband — the geometry the driver was born for, not corrected into.

— IV

An edition, not a product.

240 pieces. Numbered, signed by the thrower, accompanied by a measured certificate. When the run is finished, the run is finished.

03 · Craft

Eleven weeks, from earth to voice.

— Stage One
Throw.

A single ball of Amakusa stoneware is centered on the wheel and drawn into a tapered column over two sessions, with a one-day rest between.

Wet · ambient
— Stage Two
Bisque.

After ten days of slow drying, the piece enters the kiln for the first time. The clay hardens but remains porous, ready to receive its glaze.

First fire · 900°C
— Stage Three
Glaze.

A celadon-based glaze, reduced with iron, is applied in three pours. The driver well is masked. The base is left raw — a quiet acknowledgement of the clay below.

Brushed · poured
— Stage Four
Fire, again.

A 36-hour second firing vitrifies the body into something between stone and glass. The driver is fitted by hand, measured, signed, and shipped in cedar.

Final fire · 1,260°C
