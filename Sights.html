Nº 01 · MDCCCLXXV Begun in Paris

Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi began sculpting La Liberté éclairant le monde in 1875. The full 46-metre figure was assembled and erected on a Parisian street while construction continued — visible above the rooftops of the 17th arrondissement for nearly a decade.

Nº 02 · Frame Eiffel's iron skeleton

Inside the 2.4 mm copper skin: a wrought-iron pylon engineered by Gustave Eiffel — four tall posts braced by a hidden lattice that lets the entire statue flex up to 7 cm in a Hudson Bay storm without buckling.

Nº 03 · Crossing 350 pieces, 214 crates

Disassembled in Paris in 1885, the statue crossed the Atlantic aboard the French frigate Isère packed into 214 wooden crates. Reassembled on Bedloe's Island over four months and dedicated on the 28th of October, 1886.

Nº 04 · Patina Once copper, now verdigris

For her first 35 years she gleamed a brand-new copper orange. Oxidation set in around 1900 and by 1920 the entire surface had patinated to the iconic green — a 2.4-mm chemical reaction protecting the figure beneath for another century.

A colossus in
copper & iron.

Designed by Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi with an internal iron armature engineered by Gustave Eiffel, the Statue of Liberty was a gift from the people of France marking the centennial of American independence. Built in Paris between 1875 and 1884, disassembled into 350 pieces packed in 214 crates, then reassembled on Bedloe's Island and dedicated in October 1886. The seven rays of the crown represent the seven seas and seven continents; the tablet held in her left arm reads JULY IV MDCCLXXVI.
